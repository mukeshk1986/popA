1. Solution Design Summary
1.1 Business Purpose
The Gap Suspecting Engine identifies Medicare Advantage (MA) members who have chronic conditions that were historically documented but have not been re-captured in the current risk year. These are called "suspected gaps."
When a health plan submits risk adjustment data to CMS, every qualifying HCC (Hierarchical Condition Category) must be re-documented at least once in the measurement year. If a member's chronic condition — diabetes, heart failure, COPD, etc. — goes undocumented, the plan loses the associated Risk Adjustment revenue for that member in that year.
Gap suspecting surfaces these at-risk members and conditions to clinical teams before the RAPS/EDGE submission deadline, enabling targeted outreach, chart reviews, and care management.
Financial Impact: Each identified and re-captured HCC is worth $3,000–$8,000 in annual risk-adjusted premium per member.

4. Method 1 — Diagnosis-Based Gap Identification
4.1 Business Logic
Method 1 identifies members who had an HCC-qualifying diagnosis in a prior year but have no claim in the current risk year supporting that HCC. The logic is:
"Member had ICD code X (maps to HCC Y) in Year N-1. In Year N, there is no claim with any ICD code that maps to HCC Y. Therefore, HCC Y is a suspected gap."
4.2 Processing Steps
Step 1: Load prior-year persistence records
  ? Filter member_persistence_hcc WHERE STATUS = 'Closed'
  ? These are HCCs present before but absent in current year
  ? Filter by RISK_YEAR = current_risk_year

Step 2: Load current-year HCC assignments
  ? Filter risk_member_hcc WHERE SOURCE_LOAD_MONTH = current_month
  ? These represent already-documented HCCs

Step 3: Anti-join
  ? Prior-year HCCs NOT IN current-year HCCs
  ? JOIN key: (BID, HCC_CODE, MODEL, MODEL_VERSION)
  ? Result: candidate gaps — HCCs seen before but not this year

Step 4: Filter by method code groups
  ? Load ref_method_metadata_codegroups for METHOD_ID = 1
  ? Only retain HCCs in the configured code groups for Method 1
  ? This limits Method 1 to specific chronic condition categories

Step 5: Add method metadata
  ? Join with ref_method_metadata for METHOD_ID = 1
  ? Add METHOD_NAME, METHOD_DESCRIPTION

Step 6: Add evidence (last known diagnosis)
  ? Join with member_persistence_hcc for LAST_DOS
  ? Source DIAG_CD from the last supporting claim in persistence history

Step 7: Write to draft_gaps
  ? PARTITION (SOURCE_LOAD_MONTH, HOME_PLAN_ID_CD, METHOD_ID=1)

5. Method 2 — Procedure-Based Gap Identification
5.1 Business Logic
Method 2 identifies gaps through treatment procedures rather than diagnosis codes. The logic is:
"Member received procedure P (e.g., insulin injection, dialysis) which strongly implies condition C (e.g., Diabetes, ESRD). If no claim in the risk year contains an ICD code for condition C, it is a suspected gap."
This catches cases where providers document the treatment but omit the underlying chronic condition diagnosis code.
5.2 Processing Steps
Step 1: Load current-year claims
  ? Filter risk_member_diag for current SOURCE_LOAD_MONTH
  ? Focus on PROCEDURE_CD columns (CPT, HCPCS)

Step 2: Load Method 2 procedure-to-condition mapping
  ? From ref_method_metadata_codegroups (PROCEDURE_CODES section)
  ? Maps CPT/HCPCS procedure codes to implied HCC conditions

Step 3: Match claims to procedure groups
  ? JOIN risk_member_diag.PROCEDURE_CD with codegroups.ICD_CD
  ? Identify members who received qualifying procedures

Step 4: Check for corresponding diagnosis documentation
  ? For each member with qualifying procedure: check if supporting ICD code present
  ? Anti-join: procedures without matching ICD documentation = gap candidates

Step 5: Translate to HCC codes
  ? Map the implied condition to HCC codes using icd_hcc_mapping
  ? Filter: only HCCs not already present in risk_member_hcc

Step 6: Apply persistence filter
  ? Cross-check with member_persistence_hcc
  ? Only include if condition was documented in a prior year
  ? (New conditions without history are not gap suspects)

Step 7: Write to draft_gaps
  ? METHOD_ID = 2

6. Method 10 — Multi-Step Exclusion Logic
6.1 Business Purpose
Method 10 is the most complex and computationally intensive method. It identifies gaps through a sophisticated multi-scenario exclusion analysis with a 180-day claim lookback window.
Method 10 processes two distinct scenarios:
ScenarioDescriptionScenario 1Prior-year HCC with no supporting evidence within 180-day lookback windowScenario 2Prior-year HCC present, current-year evidence exists but fails specific validation rules6.2 Scenario 1 Processing — 180-Day Lookback
Step 1: load_method_exclusions_10
  ? Load all exclusion rules for Method 10 from ref_method_metadata_codegroups
  ? Exclusion categories:
    - EXCLUDED_HCC: HCCs exempt from Method 10 analysis
    - EXCLUDED_ICD: ICD codes that exclude the member from suspecting
    - EXCLUDED_PROC: Procedure codes that indicate gap is closed
    - EXCLUDED_FACILITY: Facility types excluded from evidence evaluation

Step 2: get_scenario1_gap_claims_optimized
  ? Look back 180 days from SOURCE_LOAD_MONTH end date
  ? Load all claims within the 180-day window
  ? Filter out claims from excluded facility types
  ? Filter out claims containing excluded ICD codes
  ? Result: valid evidence claims within window

Step 3: Anti-join with current HCC assignments
  ? For each member-HCC combination in persistence history
  ? Check if valid evidence exists within 180-day window
  ? No valid evidence = Scenario 1 gap candidate

Step 4: Apply HCC-level exclusions
  ? Remove any gaps where the HCC itself is in EXCLUDED_HCC list
  ? Remove members with EXCLUDED_ICD codes (comorbidity exclusions)

Step 5: De-duplicate scenario 1 results
  ? Same member-HCC may appear from multiple prior-year records
  ? Keep most recent LAST_DOS as evidence date
6.3 Scenario 2 Processing — Validation Rule Failures
Step 1: Identify members with some current-year evidence
  ? Members who DO have a claim in the study period for the condition
  ? But the claim fails one or more validation rules

Step 2: Apply validation rules
  ? Rule A: Evidence claim must be from valid claim type (no excluded facility types)
  ? Rule B: ICD code must be primary or secondary (not incidental)
  ? Rule C: Provider must have valid NPI (not anonymous/placeholder)
  ? Rule D: Claim must not be reversed/adjusted to zero

Step 3: Records failing any validation rule = Scenario 2 gap
  ? flag IS_SCENARIO_2 = 'Y' in draft_gaps

Step 4: Combine Scenario 1 and Scenario 2 results
  ? UNION of both scenario outputs
  ? Add METHOD_ID = 10 to all records

6.5 Method 10 Performance
Method 10 is the most resource-intensive step, historically taking up to 5 minutes on large plan datasets. The optimized implementation uses:
* Broadcast joins for exclusion tables (small reference data)
* Predicate pushdown to reduce data scanned
* Early partition filtering using SOURCE_LOAD_MONTH
* Avoidance of CROSSJOIN through proper join key design
* Optimized anti-join patterns instead of NOT EXISTS subqueries

7. Method 4 — Persistent Condition Carryforward
7.1 Business Logic
Method 4 addresses a specific scenario in MA risk adjustment:
"Member has a chronic condition that CMS considers permanently established once documented. These conditions should be automatically carried forward to the current risk year as an active gap suspect if not re-documented."
Examples: Amputation, certain neurological conditions, severe cognitive impairment. These are conditions that, once diagnosed, do not resolve — so if no claim documents them in the current year, the gap is almost certainly real.
7.2 Processing Steps
Step 1: Load permanent/persistent condition list
  ? From ref_chronic_condition WHERE IS_ANNUAL = 'N' (not annual — permanent)
  ? OR from ref_method_metadata_codegroups for METHOD_ID = 4

Step 2: Filter member_persistence_hcc for permanent conditions
  ? WHERE HCC_CODE IN (permanent condition HCC list)
  ? WHERE IS_PERSISTENT = 'Y' (confirmed in prior year)
  ? WHERE STATUS != 'Closed' (still tracked as active)

Step 3: Check current-year documentation
  ? Anti-join with risk_member_hcc
  ? Members with no current-year documentation = Method 4 gap candidates

Step 4: Apply persistence lookback
  ? DIAGNOSIS_YEAR must be within prior_years window (default: 2 years)
  ? Ensures carryforward is from recent, verified diagnoses

Step 5: Add high-confidence flag
  ? Method 4 gaps receive elevated CONFIDENCE_SCORE
  ? Permanent conditions are near-certain gaps if not re-documented

Step 6: Write to draft_gaps
  ? METHOD_ID = 4

8. Two-Level Suppression Logic
8.1 Overview
After all four methods generate draft gap records, a two-level suppression process removes false positives before the final suspected_gaps table is populated.

    ?  add_suppression1_on_draft_gap()

    ?

    ?

8.2 Suppression 1 — Documentation Evidence Check
Function: add_suppression1_on_draft_gap()
Suppression 1 marks a gap record as suppressed if documentation evidence was found in the current risk year, meaning the condition IS actually captured — the draft gap was a false positive.
For each draft_gap record (BID, HCC_CODE):

  Check: Does risk_member_hcc contain a record for this (BID, HCC_CODE)
         in the current risk year (SOURCE_LOAD_MONTH)?

  If YES: IS_SUPPRESSED_1 = 'Y' (condition is documented ? not a real gap)
  If NO:  IS_SUPPRESSED_1 = 'N' (condition is not documented ? keep as gap)
Additional Suppression 1 rules:
* If member has a claim with a higher-hierarchy HCC (HCC absorbs the suspect HCC), suppress the lower-hierarchy gap
* If the gap HCC is an interaction term already captured via another HCC path, suppress it
* If member is deceased or disenrolled before risk year end, suppress all gaps
8.3 Suppression 2 — Plan-Level Exclusions
Function: add_suppression2_on_draft_gap()
Suppression 2 marks a gap record as suppressed based on plan-specific or method-specific exclusion rules. This separates clinical exclusions from documentation status.
For each draft_gap record (BID, HCC_CODE, METHOD_ID):

  Check 1: Is this METHOD_ID excluded for this HOME_PLAN_ID_CD?
    ? Some plans may opt-out of specific gap methods
    ? IS_SUPPRESSED_2 = 'Y' if method excluded for plan

  Check 2: Is this HCC_CODE in the method's EXCLUDED_HCC list?
    ? Certain HCCs are globally excluded from suspecting
    ? E.g., HCCs with high false-positive rates
    ? IS_SUPPRESSED_2 = 'Y' if HCC excluded

  Check 3: Does the member have a contradicting ICD code?
    ? Some ICD codes indicate the condition has resolved or been superseded
    ? Load CONTRADICTING_ICD list from ref_method_metadata_codegroups
    ? IS_SUPPRESSED_2 = 'Y' if contradicting code found

  Check 4: Is the gap within an active prior authorization / care plan?
    ? If plan has flagged the member as actively being treated
    ? IS_SUPPRESSED_2 = 'Y'
8.4 two_level_suppression_logic_final()
After both suppression flags are set, this function applies the final combined filter:
def two_level_suppression_logic_final(draft_gaps_df):
    """
    Applies final suppression: a gap is suppressed if EITHER
    IS_SUPPRESSED_1 = 'Y' OR IS_SUPPRESSED_2 = 'Y'.
    
    Unsuppressed records (both = 'N') proceed to suspected_gaps.
    Suppressed records are retained in draft_gaps_with_details with flags set,
    providing full audit trail of suppression decisions.
    """
    return draft_gaps_df.withColumn(
        "IS_SUPPRESSED",
        when(
            (col("IS_SUPPRESSED_1") == "Y") | (col("IS_SUPPRESSED_2") == "Y"),
            lit("Y")
        ).otherwise(lit("N"))
    )
8.5 Suppression Audit Trail
Both suppression flags AND the final IS_SUPPRESSED column are retained in draft_gaps_with_details. This provides:
* Full audit trail for clinical review
* Ability to analyze false positive rates by method and plan
* Input for suppression rule tuning

9. Frequency and Confidence Scoring
9.1 Frequency Calculation
Function: save_to_suspected_gaps_with_frequency()
Frequency counts how many distinct methods identified the same member-HCC gap:
Member A, HCC 19 (Diabetes):
  ? Identified by Method 1 (prior-year persistence):    COUNT +1
  ? Identified by Method 10 Scenario 1 (180-day):      COUNT +1
  ? Not identified by Method 2 (no procedure evidence):  --
  ? Not identified by Method 4 (not permanent):          --
  
FREQUENCY = 2
A higher frequency score means multiple independent methods corroborate the gap, increasing clinical confidence.
def save_to_suspected_gaps_with_frequency(
    spark, catalog, gap_schema_curation, schema_curation, ma_reference,
    risk_year, source_load_year_month, plan_name, current_user, logger
):
    """
    Aggregates draft_gaps_with_details by (BID, HCC_CODE, MODEL, MODEL_VERSION).
    Computes:
      - FREQUENCY: count of distinct METHOD_IDs that flagged this gap
      - CONFIDENCE_SCORE: normalized frequency-based confidence
      - LAST_DOS: most recent date of service across all methods
    Writes final record to suspected_gaps.
    """
9.2 Confidence Score Computation
CONFIDENCE_SCORE = FREQUENCY / MAX_POSSIBLE_METHODS

Where MAX_POSSIBLE_METHODS = number of active methods in method_list parameter.

Example:
  method_list = [1, 2, 10, 4]  ? MAX = 4
  FREQUENCY = 2                 ? CONFIDENCE = 2/4 = 0.50

  FREQUENCY = 4                 ? CONFIDENCE = 4/4 = 1.00 (all methods agree)
  FREQUENCY = 1                 ? CONFIDENCE = 1/4 = 0.25 (single signal)
9.3 Suspected Gaps Aggregation Logic
When writing to suspected_gaps, the engine aggregates at the member-HCC level:
GROUP BY (BID, MEMBER_ID, HOME_PLAN_ID_CD, HCC_CODE, MODEL, MODEL_VERSION, RISK_YEAR)

AGGREGATE:
  FREQUENCY        = COUNT(DISTINCT METHOD_ID)
  CONFIDENCE_SCORE = FREQUENCY / MAX_METHODS
  LAST_DOS         = MAX(LAST_DOS across all methods)
  PROVIDER_NPI     = FIRST(PROVIDER_NPI) from most-recent method evidence
  DIAG_CD_LIST     = COLLECT_SET(DIAG_CD) — all supporting ICD codes
  METHOD_LIST      = COLLECT_SET(METHOD_ID) — which methods identified it

10. risk_member_hcc Integration
10.1 Why risk_member_hcc Matters for Gap Suspecting
risk_member_hcc is the output of the Risk Engine scoring models. For gap suspecting, it serves two roles:
1. Current-year documentation evidence: If a member has an HCC in risk_member_hcc for the current SOURCE_LOAD_MONTH, that HCC is NOT a gap (it's been captured).
2. Method-tagged HCC tracking: The METHOD_ID column in risk_member_hcc links each HCC assignment back to which gap suspecting method drove its identification.
10.2 HCC-to-Gap Feedback Loop
Risk Engine Run #1 (initial):
  ? cms_hcc_risk_score_calc produces risk_member_hcc (METHOD_ID = NULL)
  
Gap Suspecting Run:
  ? gap_suspecting_main identifies gaps, writes to suspected_gaps
  ? Updates risk_member_hcc with METHOD_ID for newly-suspected conditions
  
Risk Engine Run #2 (after gap capture):
  ? cms_hcc_risk_score_calc includes gap-sourced diagnoses
  ? risk_member_hcc updated with method-tagged records
  ? suspected_gaps suppressed for now-documented conditions (IS_SUPPRESSED_1 = 'Y')

12. Performance Characteristics and Optimization
12.1 Scale
The gap suspecting engine operates on datasets of:
MetricValueTotal records (draft_gaps across methods)400–500+ millionMembers per large plan500,000–2,000,000HCC codes per member3–15 averageMethods per run4 (1, 2, 4, 10)Processing time (POP-ADVYZER-LARGE)30–90 minutes per plan12.2 Partition Strategy
All three gap output tables use a 3-column partition:
PARTITIONED BY (SOURCE_LOAD_MONTH, HOME_PLAN_ID_CD, METHOD_ID)
Rationale:
* SOURCE_LOAD_MONTH: Enables efficient monthly reprocessing (partition overwrite)
* HOME_PLAN_ID_CD: Isolates plan data for targeted queries and rewrites
* METHOD_ID: Allows independent method reprocessing without touching other methods
Real-world partition size variation:
PartitionApprox Record CountSmall plan + Method 4~145 recordsLarge plan + Method 10~26.7 million recordsThis extreme variation (145 to 26.7M) is inherent to the data distribution and reflects:
* Method 4's precision (permanent conditions only = low volume)
* Method 10's comprehensiveness (all chronic conditions with exclusion logic = high volume)
* Plan size differences (small vs. large plan membership)

12.4 Performance Optimization Techniques
Method 10 get_scenario1_gap_claims_optimized:
1. Push SOURCE_LOAD_MONTH filter before all joins (partition pruning)
2. Broadcast-join exclusion tables (ref_method_metadata_codegroups is <100MB)
3. Use anti-join instead of NOT IN subquery
4. Cache persistence DataFrame (reused in Scenario 1 and Scenario 2)
5. Avoid unnecessary column selection — select only required columns early
save_to_draft_gaps_table optimization:
1. Repartition before write: df.repartition(200, "HOME_PLAN_ID_CD", "METHOD_ID")
2. Use partition overwrite: spark.conf.set("spark.sql.sources.partitionOverwriteMode", "dynamic")
3. Write mode: "overwrite" scoped to current partition
4. Enable optimizeWrite at session level for current run
Suppression logic optimization:
1. Cache draft_gaps DataFrame (read twice for suppression 1 and 2)
2. Suppression 1: use broadcast join with risk_member_hcc current partition
3. Suppression 2: minimize ref_method_metadata_codegroups reloads (cache once)
4. Two-level suppression combined in single pass (avoid reading draft_gaps twice)
